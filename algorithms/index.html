    Algorithms

    Recovery from folded samples amounts to identifying the integer number of folds at each sample. Our algorithms address this task at low sampling rates and under noise, for single-channel and multi-channel systems. Most of them have also been evaluated on data acquired with our hardware prototype.

    Single-Channel

    Difference-Based Recovery (RSoD)

    Problem Existing single-channel methods require a high oversampling factor (OF ≥ 17), fail under noise because high-order differences amplify it by up to 2N, or rely on iterative optimization. Approach Recovery from second-order differences (RSoD) differences the folded samples twice, folds the result, and sums it back with an order-matched anti-differencing window. The method is in closed form and requires no iterations. Result Recovery from OF ≈ 8–10 in noisy simulations, a runtime of approximately 1.5 ms, up to 200 times shorter than that of optimization-based methods, and recovery of a hardware signal at a folding-depth ratio of 108.

    On the same hardware record (ρ = 102), RSoD reaches 23.4 dB, whereas three existing methods remain below 6.4 dB even at higher sampling rates.

    Difference-Based Recovery for Modulo Sampling: Tightened Bounds and Robustness Guarantees
    Wenyi Yan, Zeyuan Li, Lu Gan, Hongqing Liu, Guoquan Li
    IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing  ·  01 Jan 2026  ·  doi:10.1109/TSP.2026.3696002

    Robustness to Noise and Jitter

    A lower difference order amplifies noise less, and the recovery guarantee extends to sampling-clock jitter.

    Theorem (recovery under sampling jitter)

    Let the sampling instants be $t_k = kT + \mu_k$ with $|\mu_k| \le \nu T$, $\nu \in [0,1)$. With $\rho$ and $\rho_\eta$ as before, if

    \[\rho\left[\left(2\sin\frac{T\Omega}{2}\right)^{N} + 2^{N+1}\sin\frac{\nu T\Omega}{2}\right] + 2^{N}\rho_\eta < 1,\]

    the algorithm returns $\hat g[k] = g[k] + \eta[k] + 2m\lambda$ for a constant integer $m$.

    Interpretation. Jitter adds a term that grows linearly with $\nu$. For $\nu = 0$, the condition reduces to the jitter-free condition on the Theory page.

    On hardware, a signal sampled with 9% jitter is recovered with an SNR of 27.4 dB, and an input with 15 dB SNR with 29.3 dB.

    Learning-Based Recovery (CoFMo)

    Problem At low oversampling (OF ≈ 3–5) and large folding-depth ratios, adjacent samples may differ by several folds, and analytical methods cannot distinguish the fold counts. Noise, 4-bit quantization, and hardware nonidealities increase this ambiguity. Existing learning-based methods regress the waveform directly without using the integer fold structure and have been evaluated only on synthetic data. Approach CoFMo is a coarse-to-fine network. It classifies the integer fold count of every sample with absolute and relative classification heads coupled by a consistency loss, unfolds the samples in closed form, and then regresses the residual. Its input consists of the modulo samples and their first-order difference, and a gated temporo-spectral module combines a Wiener-like spectral gate with self-attention to capture the long-range folding history. Five loss terms are weighted by learned uncertainty parameters. Result The widest successful-recovery region among four analytical methods and the learning-based MUNet. On measured 64-sample records from our prototype with injected noise and 4-bit quantization, the normalized mean-square error (NMSE) is −19.45 to −29.64 dB at OF 2.77–5.53, which is 7.81–10.89 dB lower than that of MUNet, whereas every analytical baseline remains near 0 dB. To our knowledge, it is the first learning-based modulo recovery method validated on physical modulo ADC measurements.

    Decoupled coarse-to-fine recovery

    The measured samples are $y_{\mathrm e}[n] = x[n] - 2\lambda k[n] + \varepsilon[n]$, so an error in the integer $k[n]$ causes an error of at least $2\lambda$, whereas $\varepsilon[n]$ causes only a bounded residual. CoFMo therefore separates the estimate into a discrete and a continuous part,

    \[\hat x[n] = \underbrace{y_{\mathrm e}[n] + 2\lambda\,\hat k[n]}_{x_{\mathrm{coarse}}[n]} + \hat r[n],\qquad \hat k[n] = \arg\max_{i\in\{-K_{\max},\dots,K_{\max}\}} p_{k,i}[n].\]

    The fold budgets follow from the signal model, since $|k[n]| \le \lfloor(\rho+1)/2\rfloor$ and, by Bernstein's inequality,

    \[|\Delta k\[n]| < \frac{\pi\rho}{2\,\mathrm{OF}} + 1.\]

    Interpretation. Each fold count is selected from a finite set of integers rather than regressed as a real number, so an isolated misclassification does not propagate as a sequence of $2\lambda$ offsets along the record.

    The cost is approximately 1.1 GFLOPs and 6 ms per 512-sample sequence on a GPU. Inference is a fixed forward pass, so the cost does not depend on the folding-depth ratio. Recovery is currently performed offline, and an FPGA implementation on the prototype is planned.

    Decoupled Coarse-to-Fine Learning for Modulo Signal Recovery
    Yutao Jin, Wenyi Yan, Zichen Wang, Hongqing Liu, Guoquan Li, Lu Gan
    IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement  ·  02 Nov 2026  ·  Accepted for publication

    Multi-Channel

    Robust CRT with Signal Unwrapping

    Problem The RCRT recovers inputs only within its recoverable range P. Beyond this range, the folding integers of the channels can no longer be matched. Approach The two approaches are cascaded. The RCRT combines the channels into one virtual modulo signal with a larger effective threshold, and single-channel RSoD then unwraps this signal. Result On hardware, a signal with a peak of 5.04 V, 3.5 times the RCRT recoverable range of 1.44 V, is recovered with an SNR of 28.8 dB.

    Effective threshold

    The RCRT stage acts as a single modulo ADC with

    \[\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}} = \varepsilon\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\tau_\ell = 2P,\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{\rho}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\tau_\ell},\]

    so the unwrapping stage operates at a folding-depth ratio reduced by the factor $\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\tau_\ell$.

    Integrating Robust CRT With Signal Unwrapping for Modulo Sampling
    Chutong Shen, Wenyi Yan, Yimin D. Zhang, Lu Gan
    IEEE Signal Processing Letters  ·  01 Jan 2026  ·  doi:10.1109/LSP.2026.3696895

    Threshold Sensitivity Stabilization Algorithm (TSSA)

    Problem The recoverable range and the error tolerance of the RCRT depend on the lcm and the gcd of the two thresholds, so a threshold deviation of 0.001 can change them by orders of magnitude. With Δ2 = 0.16, a deviation of Δ1 from 0.14 to 0.141 enlarges the recoverable range from ±0.56 to ±11.28 but reduces the error tolerance by a factor of 20, from 5 × 10⁻³ to 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ (see Threshold Deviation). Approach The TSSA trades recoverable range for a stable error tolerance. It identifies the folding integers from the remainder difference and the threshold spacing σ = Δ2 − Δ1, with the deviated thresholds obtained by calibration. No modular inverse is required, which keeps the implementation cost low. Result Across all tested deviations, the error tolerance of the TSSA remains between 3.3 × 10⁻³ and 6.8 × 10⁻³, whereas that of the RCRT falls to 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ or 5 × 10⁻⁵. Under 2–12-bit quantization, the TSSA with deviated thresholds performs as well as the RCRT with the designed thresholds.

    Theorem (TSSA recoverable range and error tolerance)

    Consider a two-channel modulo ADC with thresholds $\Delta_1 < \Delta_2 < 2\Delta_1$ and let $\sigma = \Delta_2 - \Delta_1$.

    (a) For signal amplitudes within $[-\bar P, \bar P]$, where

    \[\bar P = \max\left\{\Delta_2\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\Delta_2}{2\sigma}\right\rfloor - 0.5\right),\\ \Delta_1\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\Delta_1}{2\sigma}\right\rfloor + 0.5\right)\right\},\]

    the TSSA attains the error tolerance $\bar\delta = \sigma/4$.

    (b) If $\Delta_2/2 < \Delta_1 < 2\Delta_2/3$, then $\bar P = \Delta_2/2$.

    (c) If $\Delta_1 = q\epsilon$ and $\Delta_2 = (q+1)\epsilon$ with integer $q \ge 2$, then $\bar P = P = q(q+1)\epsilon/2$ and $\bar\delta = \delta = \epsilon/4$, i.e., the TSSA attains the recoverable range and the error tolerance of the RCRT.

    Interpretation. The error tolerance depends only on the threshold spacing, so it changes continuously under threshold deviation, whereas the RCRT tolerance changes with the gcd of the thresholds. By part (b), $\Delta_1$ should exceed $2\Delta_2/3$, since otherwise the second channel alone covers the recoverable range.

    Wenyi Yan, Lu Gan, Yimin D. Zhang

    On-Chip Real-Time Reconstruction

    Problem Modulo recovery is usually performed offline in software, which precludes real-time operation. Approach With consecutive moduli, the modular inverse has a closed form, so the RCRT reduces to four fixed arithmetic steps that map onto a streaming FPGA pipeline. Result Folding and reconstruction on one FPGA with a fixed latency of 51 clock cycles and a throughput of one sample per clock cycle, using less than 3% of the device (1,200 LUTs and 2 DSP slices) and less than 0.7 W of dynamic power.

    Corollary (inverse-free RCRT)

    If $\tau_2 = \tau_1 + 1$, then $\tau_1^{-1} \equiv \tau_1 \pmod{\tau_2}$, and the reconstruction becomes

    \[\hat q = \left\lfloor \frac{\hat y_2 - \hat y_1}{\varepsilon} \right\rceil,\quad \hat n_1 = \langle \hat q\,\tau_1 \rangle_{\tau_2},\quad \hat n_2 = \frac{\hat n_1\tau_1 - \hat q}{\tau_2},\quad \hat g = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\left(\hat n_\ell\Delta_\ell + \hat y_\ell\right).\]
