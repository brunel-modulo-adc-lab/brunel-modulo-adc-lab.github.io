    Hardware

    An FPGA-calibrated modulo ADC prototype with up to four channels, which extends the input range by a factor of more than 100.

    Problem Analog folding loops become unstable at large folding-depth ratios, since the threshold error accumulates with every fold and the circuit enters limit cycles. Earlier prototypes were therefore limited to small folding-depth ratios. Approach A comparator-triggered folding loop is controlled by a finite-state machine on a Zynq-7000 FPGA at 200 MHz. A 14-bit DAC sets the threshold between 100 and 500 mV with a deviation within ±0.2%, and a calibration converts the error that accumulates with the folding depth into a constant offset. Result Stable folding beyond ρ = 100. With a 3-bit ADC, the SINAD is 37.5 dB higher and the effective number of bits (ENOB) 6.2 bits higher than those of a conventional ADC with the same resolution, close to the theoretical value of log₂108 ≈ 6.75 bits. The control logic consumes less than 10 mW.

    Architecture

    Calibration

    Comparator and DAC errors cause the threshold to deviate from its designed value, and the spread of this deviation more than doubles as ρ increases from 1.6 to 2.9. A controlled under-compensation subtracts a known step at every fold, so the residual error remains constant as the folding-depth ratio increases.

    Results

    • Input range. A 10.8 V signal is acquired with a 0.1 V threshold (ρ = 108) and recovered with a SINAD of 57.1 dB using a 3-bit ADC.
    • Noise floor. The in-band noise is approximately 20 dB lower than that of a conventional ADC at ρ = 92.
    • Bandwidth. Signals up to 400 kHz are recovered at smaller folding-depth ratios, with a reconstruction SNR of 22–33 dB.
    • Modulated signals. QAM, BPSK, and FSK waveforms are folded and recovered on hardware.
    • Learning-based recovery. CoFMo recovers measured 64-sample records at OF 2.77–5.53 with 4-bit quantization and an NMSE of −19.45 to −29.64 dB (see Algorithms).
    • Real-time operation. Two-channel folding and RCRT reconstruction run on the FPGA with a latency of 51 clock cycles (see Algorithms).
